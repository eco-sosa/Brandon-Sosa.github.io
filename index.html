I’m Brandon Sosa am a PhD candidate in the Tropical Fish Ecology Lab.

My research bridges ecological fieldwork, economic valuation, and dynamic modeling to support large-scale reef recovery, in the Florida Keys. I focus on the practical use of marine grazers like urchins and crabs to reduce algae and boost reef restoration success.

About Me

I was born and raised in Hialeah, Florida (outskirts of Miami). Growing up in a dense urban environment, I connected with nature through camping in the Everglades, participating in beach cleanups along the mangrove islands, and eventually diving into the world of marine science.

Academic Journey

I started at Purdue University on a Wildlife pre-veterinary track before a Benjamin A. Gilman International Scholarship took me to the University of Queensland. There, I worked with Dr. Robert Mason in the Spatial Marine Ecology Lab on a review of boat grounding and anchoring impacts on coral reefs. A UQ symposium showcasing collaborative reef science convinced me to pursue marine ecology.

Research Highlight: I had the fortune of conducting small research projects in Australia at Heron Island in 2020 (Southern GBR) and Lizard Island in 2025 (Northern GBR)

Back at Purdue, I worked as a research technician in the Höök Lab, assisting in a study on microplastic trophic transfer in freshwater fish (see publication). I later led a meta-analysis on mercury and PCB bioaccumulation and maternal transfer effects on fish offspring (manuscript in revision, 09/2025 Fish and Fisheries).

Current Research at FIU

Today, I’m pursuing a Ph.D. in Natural Resource Science and Management at Florida International University, blending ecological research with economic and social insight. I was mentored by Dr. Mark Butler , whose pioneering work in marine invertebrate ecology shaped my academic foundation. I am now co-advised by:

  • Dr. Mahadev Bhat - Environmental economist focused on natural resource management
  • Dr. Alastair Harborne - Coral reef ecologist specializing in fish-habitat interactions and reef resilience

My current research focuses on how marine grazers — like Caribbean King Crabs and long-spined sea urchins — can help reduce algae and enhance coral restoration success in the Florida Keys. I integrate field ecology, mariculture, ecological modeling, and social survey methods to understand which strategies work best, and how to communicate their value to the public and managers.

Beyond the Dissertation

Beyond my dissertation, I’ve collaborated with the Everglades Foundation to support a total ecosystem service valuation of the Greater Everglades system. This work has included the development of benefit-transfer approaches and identifying critical knowledge gaps in how we value wetlands, fisheries, water quality, and coastal resilience.
